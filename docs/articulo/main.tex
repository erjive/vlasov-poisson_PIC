\title{Phase space mixing in the presence of self-gravity: spherically symmetric Vlasov-Poisson evolutions from initial data out of equilibrium}

\author{Erik Jim\'enez-V\'azquez}

\begin{abstract}
\EJ{Provisional abstract; to be rewritten when sections~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} to~\ref{Sec:DistributedL} are final. The statements about a critical value of $\mu$ below are outdated: the change between $\mu = 0.43$ and $0.48$ is continuous, and the oscillation of the core is not damped for $\mu = 0.43$ (section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq}).}
These notes are devoted to the numerical study of a spherically symmetric, collisionless kinetic gas which is trapped in an external central potential, whose self-gravity is taken into account, and whose initial distribution function is concentrated about one orbital phase, far from any steady state. In the absence of self-gravity, such a gas relaxes in time to a stationary state through phase space mixing. We analyze how this relaxation is modified by the self-gravity of the gas, for the reduced model in which all the gas particles have the same magnitude of the angular momentum, by means of a particle-in-cell code whose particles are placed on a regular lattice in action-angle variables. The outcome is controlled by a parameter $\mu$ which compares the amplitude of the potential generated by the gas with the spread of its orbital frequencies. For small $\mu$ the gas mixes, more slowly than in the absence of self-gravity, and the potential which it generates relaxes to a stationary profile, as in Landau damping; however, the state which is reached differs from the average of the initial distribution function over the angle variables. For larger $\mu$ a compact part of the gas survives as a dense core at the circular orbit, whose radial oscillation is damped. Above a critical value of $\mu$ this oscillation persists, and the potential does not relax. For clouds which are compact in angle the critical value lies between $0.43$ and $0.48$, both in the isochrone and in the Kepler potential.
\end{abstract}
