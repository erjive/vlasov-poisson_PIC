\begin{abstract}
\EJ{Provisional abstract; to be rewritten when section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} is final.}
These notes are devoted to the numerical study of a spherically symmetric, collisionless kinetic gas which is trapped in an external central potential, whose self-gravity is taken into account, and whose initial distribution function is concentrated about one orbital phase, far from any steady state. In the absence of self-gravity, such a gas relaxes in time to a stationary state through phase space mixing. We analyze how this relaxation is modified by the self-gravity of the gas, for the reduced model in which all the gas particles have the same magnitude of the angular momentum, by means of a particle-in-cell code whose particles are placed on a regular lattice in action-angle variables. The outcome is controlled by a parameter $\mu$ which compares the amplitude of the potential generated by the gas with the spread of its orbital frequencies. Below a critical value of $\mu$ the gas mixes, more slowly than in the absence of self-gravity, and the potential which it generates relaxes to a stationary profile, as in Landau damping. Above this value it collapses into a compact, self-bound clump which oscillates radially and does not mix. For clouds which are compact in angle the critical value lies between $0.43$ and $0.48$, both in the isochrone and in the Kepler potential.
\end{abstract}
