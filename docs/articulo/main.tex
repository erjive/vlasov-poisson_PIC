\title{Phase space mixing in the presence of self-gravity: spherically symmetric Vlasov-Poisson evolutions from initial data out of equilibrium}

\author{Erik Jim\'enez-V\'azquez}
\affiliation{\EJ{Affiliation to be completed.}}

\author{Olivier Sarbach}
\affiliation{Instituto de F\'isica y Matem\'aticas,
Universidad Michoacana de San Nicol\'as de Hidalgo,
Edificio C-3, Ciudad Universitaria, 58040 Morelia, Michoac\'an, M\'exico}


\begin{abstract}
\EJ{Draft of 15 November 2026, to be revised after the review of sections~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} to~\ref{Sec:DistributedL}.}
These notes are devoted to the numerical study of a spherically symmetric, collisionless kinetic gas which is trapped in an external central potential, whose self-gravity is taken into account, and whose initial distribution function is concentrated about one orbital phase, far from any steady state. In the absence of self-gravity, such a gas relaxes to a stationary state through phase space mixing. We analyze how this relaxation changes when the mass of the gas is increased, for the reduced model in which all the gas particles have the same magnitude of the angular momentum and for the isochrone and the Kepler potentials, by means of a particle-in-cell code whose particles are placed on a regular lattice in action-angle variables. For clouds of a given shape, the evolution is controlled by a parameter $\mu$ which compares the change of the orbital frequencies due to the self-gravity with their spread. For small $\mu$ the gas mixes and the potential which it generates relaxes, more slowly than in the absence of self-gravity, which is the analogue of Landau damping; however, gas accumulates near the circular orbit, and the state which is reached differs from the average of the initial distribution function over the angle variables. For $\mu\gtrsim 0.4$ part of the gas survives as a dense core at the circular orbit, whose radial oscillation is not damped within our simulations. The change between these two behaviors is steep but continuous, and we find no critical value of $\mu$. We derive a model in which the interaction of the gas is averaged over the radial oscillations of nearly circular orbits. It describes the gas as a two-dimensional fluid in the plane of the complex amplitudes of these oscillations, it reproduces the simulations without any adjustable parameter, and it explains why the core forms at the circular orbit and with which frequency it oscillates. According to the model, the core consists of gas which the rotation induced by the cloud carries to the circular orbit before the differential rotation of the orbits winds the cloud up. Whether this happens is decided by a parameter $\Gamma$ which depends on the distance of the cloud from the circular orbit, not on the spread of its frequencies: clouds of different shapes in the action variable, including a narrow ring and clouds which do not reach the circular orbit, form a core at the same value of $\Gamma$ but at values of $\mu$ which differ by up to a factor three, as we confirm with simulations whose outcome was predicted in advance. Finally, when the angular momenta of the gas particles are distributed, a relative spread of the order of one percent, which grows with the mass of the gas, removes the core, and the gas mixes.
\end{abstract}
