\begin{abstract}
\EJ{Provisional title and abstract; to be rewritten when sections~\ref{Sec:FixedL} and~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} are final.}
These notes are devoted to the numerical study of a spherically symmetric, collisionless kinetic gas which is trapped in an external central potential, whose self-gravity is taken into account, and whose initial distribution function is not a steady state. In the absence of self-gravity, such a gas relaxes in time to a stationary state through phase space mixing. We analyze how this relaxation is modified by the self-gravity of the gas, for the reduced model in which all the gas particles have the same magnitude of the angular momentum. Our study is based on a particle-in-cell code, on a numerical solution of the linearized equations in action-angle variables, and on a spectral criterion which characterizes the existence of oscillating modes. For initial data close to a self-consistent steady state, the deviation from that state is damped unless the steady state admits an oscillating mode, which happens above a threshold mass that is accurately predicted by the spectral criterion, and at late times the self-gravity slows down the algebraic decay of the observables. For initial data far from equilibrium, the gas mixes when its self-gravity is weak and collapses into a self-bound clump which does not mix when the ratio between the bounce frequency in its potential well and the spread of its orbital frequencies exceeds a critical value.
\end{abstract}
