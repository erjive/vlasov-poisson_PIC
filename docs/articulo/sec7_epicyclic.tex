\section{A model for the gas near the circular orbit}
\label{Sec:Epicyclic}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

In this section we derive a model for the gas cloud of section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} which explains the results found there. It rests on two observations. First, the orbits of the cloud have a small eccentricity, such that all the gas shells stay close to the radius of the circular orbit, and two such shells attract each other like two parallel sheets. Second, the self-gravity of the cloud and the differences between the orbital frequencies change the radial oscillations of the shells slowly compared with their period, such that the interaction can be averaged over this oscillation. We refer to the resulting model as the epicyclic model.\EJ{In the notes in docs/modelo\_reducido this model is called ``the reduced model''; since these notes use that name for the model with fixed angular momentum (section~\ref{Sec:VPSphSym}), we propose ``the epicyclic model''. To be decided by the authors.} We derive it in section~\ref{SubSec:EpiDerivation}. For a cloud of a given shape it contains the single parameter $\Gamma$ of Eq.~(\ref{Eq:GammaDef}) (section~\ref{SubSec:EpiParameter}), and it gives the frequencies of the mixed states and of the oscillation of the core in closed form (section~\ref{SubSec:EpiFrequencies}). Without adjusting any parameter, its solutions reproduce the PIC simulations of section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} (section~\ref{SubSec:EpiComparison}). In this section all the gas particles have the same angular momentum $L_0$; the extension to distributed angular momenta is the subject of section~\ref{Sec:DistributedL}.


\subsection{Derivation}
\label{SubSec:EpiDerivation}

The computational particles of the PIC method are spherical shells, and their equations of motion~(\ref{Eq:EOM}) also describe a gas of $N$ shells with radii $r_j$, radial momenta $p_j$, angular momenta $L_j$ and masses $m_j$. Since $\partial\Phi_{gas}/\partial r(t,r_j) = G m(t,r_j)/r_j^2$, with $m(t,r)$ the mass of the gas inside the radius $r$, Eqs.~(\ref{Eq:EOM}) are Hamilton's equations for the function
\begin{equation}
H = \sum\limits_{j=1}^N m_j\left[ \frac{p_j^2}{2} + \frac{L_j^2}{2r_j^2} + \Phi_{ext}(r_j) \right]
 - G\sum\limits_{i<j} \frac{m_i m_j}{\max\{ r_i,r_j \}}
\label{Eq:ShellH}
\end{equation}
with respect to the canonical pairs $(r_j,m_j p_j)$: the derivative of the last term with respect to $r_i$ yields the mass of the shells which lie inside the shell $i$, divided by $r_i^2$.

Let $R_0$ be the radius of the circular orbit with angular momentum $L_0$ in the external potential, let $\kappa_0 := \Omega(0,L_0)$ be the frequency of the small radial oscillations about it, and write $E(J)$ and $\Omega(J)$ for $E(J,L_0)$ and $\Omega(J,L_0)$. For nearly circular orbits
\begin{equation}
r = R_0 - \left( \frac{2J}{\kappa_0} \right)^{1/2}\cos Q + \mathcal{O}(J),\qquad
p_r = \left( 2\kappa_0 J \right)^{1/2}\sin Q + \mathcal{O}(J),
\label{Eq:Epicycle}
\end{equation}
with $Q = 0$ at the pericenter. For the isochrone potential with $L_0 = 2$ one has $R_0 = 5.742$ and $\kappa_0 = 0.07107$, and for the Kepler potential $R_0 = L_0^2 = 4$ and $\kappa_0 = L_0^{-3} = 1/8$, see Eq.~(\ref{Eq:OmegaIsochrone}).

Suppose that all the shells stay close to $R_0$, that is, $r_j = R_0 + \xi_j$ with $|\xi_j|\ll R_0$. Using $\max\{ a,b \} = (a + b + |a - b|)/2$, the interaction energy of two shells is
\begin{equation}
-\frac{G m_i m_j}{\max\{ r_i,r_j \}} = -\frac{G m_i m_j}{R_0}
 + \frac{G m_i m_j}{2R_0^2}\left( \xi_i + \xi_j \right)
 + \frac{G m_i m_j}{2R_0^2}\left| \xi_i - \xi_j \right|
 + \mathcal{O}\left( \frac{\xi^2}{R_0^3} \right).
\label{Eq:PairExpansion}
\end{equation}
The first term on the right-hand side is a constant. The second one exerts the same force on all the shells, as if half of the mass of the remaining gas was located at the center. It displaces the circular orbits inwards and shifts all the frequencies by the same amount (section~\ref{SubSec:EpiFrequencies}), such that it does not affect the motion of the shells relative to each other. The third term is the interaction energy of two parallel sheets: it gives rise to a force between two shells which does not depend on their distance and which changes its sign when they cross. This term contains the collective dynamics of the cloud.

We describe the radial oscillation of the shell $j$ by its complex amplitude in a frame which rotates with the frequency $\kappa_0$,
\begin{equation}
\zeta_j := \sqrt{2J_j}\, e^{i(Q_j - \kappa_0 t)},
\label{Eq:zetaDef}
\end{equation}
where $(Q_j,J_j)$ are the action-angle variables of the external potential. According to Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Epicycle}), $\zeta_j e^{i\kappa_0 t} = -\sqrt{\kappa_0}\,(r_j - R_0) + i p_j/\sqrt{\kappa_0} + \mathcal{O}(J)$, such that $\zeta_j$ describes the position of the shell in the $(r,p_r)$-plane relative to the circular orbit, after a rescaling of the axes. In the absence of self-gravity $J_j$ is constant and $\dot{\zeta}_j = i[\Omega(J_j) - \kappa_0]\zeta_j$: the shells turn about the circular orbit $\zeta = 0$ with an angular velocity which depends on their distance from it. This differential rotation is responsible for the mixing. Since $\xi_i - \xi_j = -\kappa_0^{-1/2}\re[(\zeta_i - \zeta_j)e^{i\kappa_0 t}] + \mathcal{O}(J)$, if the amplitudes change little during one period $2\pi/\kappa_0$, the interaction can be replaced with its average over one period at fixed amplitudes. The average of $|\cos\theta|$ over $\theta$ is $2/\pi$, and thus
\begin{equation}
\left\langle \left| \xi_i - \xi_j \right| \right\rangle = \frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{\kappa_0}}\left| \zeta_i - \zeta_j \right|.
\label{Eq:AverageFixedL}
\end{equation}
Omitting the first two terms of Eq.~(\ref{Eq:PairExpansion}), the Hamiltonian~(\ref{Eq:ShellH}) is replaced with
\begin{equation}
\overline{H} = \sum\limits_{j=1}^N m_j\left[ E(J_j) - \kappa_0 J_j \right] + g\sum\limits_{i<j} m_i m_j\left| \zeta_i - \zeta_j \right|,\qquad
g := \frac{G}{\pi R_0^2\sqrt{\kappa_0}},
\label{Eq:Hepi}
\end{equation}
where $J_j = |\zeta_j|^2/2$ and the term $-\kappa_0 J_j$ is due to the rotating frame. The canonical pairs are $(\vartheta_j,m_j J_j)$, with $\vartheta_j := Q_j - \kappa_0 t$ the argument of $\zeta_j$. With $\zeta_j = x_j + i y_j$ and $h_j := \partial\overline{H}/\partial m_j$ at fixed phase-space coordinates, Hamilton's equations are equivalent to $\dot{\zeta}_j = i(\partial_{x_j} + i\partial_{y_j})h_j$, that is,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\zeta_i}{dt} = i\left[ \Omega(J_i) - \kappa_0 \right]\zeta_i
 + i g\sum\limits_{j\neq i} m_j\frac{\zeta_i - \zeta_j}{|\zeta_i - \zeta_j|},\qquad i = 1,2,\ldots,N.
\label{Eq:Epicyclic}
\end{equation}
These are the equations of the epicyclic model. Besides $\overline{H}$ they conserve $P := \sum_j m_j J_j$, since $\overline{H}$ is invariant with respect to a common rotation of all the amplitudes. For the isochrone potential with $L_0 = 2$ one has $g = 0.03621$.

The two terms on the right-hand side of Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Epicyclic}) have a simple meaning. The first one is the differential rotation due to the external potential: since $\Omega$ decreases with $J$, the angular velocity $\Omega(J) - \kappa_0$ vanishes at the circular orbit and is negative elsewhere. The second one is a rotation which each shell induces on the other shells, about itself and in the positive sense, with the speed $g m_j$, which does not depend on the distance. In the limit $N\to\infty$ the gas is described by its mass density $n(t,\zeta)$ in the $\zeta$-plane, which is transported by the area-preserving flow with the stream function
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}(t,\zeta) = E(J) - \kappa_0 J + g\int \left| \zeta - \zeta' \right| n(t,\zeta')\, d^2\zeta'.
\label{Eq:StreamFunction}
\end{equation}
This is the structure of the equation for the vorticity of a two-dimensional incompressible fluid, with the kernel $|\zeta - \zeta'|$ in place of the logarithmic one. Two consequences follow directly. First, a concentration of gas at $\zeta = 0$, that is, at the circular orbit, is not sheared by the differential rotation, which explains why the core of section~\ref{SubSec:FarLate} is located there. Second, a compact cloud at a distance from the origin rotates about its own center, and this rotation transports the part of the cloud which leads in the angle variable towards the origin.

The interaction in Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Hepi}) is the one of the self-gravitating sheet model, which leads to quasi-stationary states with a dense core and a dilute halo in one-dimensional systems~\cite{mJtW11,yLrPfRtTfB14}, and which also describes the motion of the stars perpendicular to the plane of a galactic disk; in that context the self-gravity of the disk has been found to delay the phase mixing of a perturbation~\cite{kDlW19,lW23,tAtA26}. For two-dimensional fluids, the damping of an asymmetry of a vortex through its resonance with the co-rotating fluid and the motion of a vortex in a background flow have been analyzed in Refs.~\cite{dSdDaCcDiLtO00,dSdD01}.

The derivation assumes that (i) the eccentricity of the orbits is small, the relevant parameter being the ratio between the amplitude of the radial oscillation and $R_0$, (ii) $|\Omega(J) - \kappa_0|\ll\kappa_0$ over the support of the cloud, and (iii) the rotation which is induced by the gas, of the order of $g M_{gas}/|\zeta|$, is slow compared with $\kappa_0$. For the reference cloud of section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} the ratio in (i) is $(2\sigma_J/\kappa_0)^{1/2}/R_0 = 0.29$, the one in (ii) is $0.12$ at $J = \sigma_J$ and $0.28$ at the lower end of the frequency band, and the one in (iii) is $0.02$ for $M_{gas}/M = 0.02$. Hence, the first assumption is the most restrictive one. In Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Epicyclic}) we keep the exact frequency $\Omega(J)$ of the external potential, such that the model reproduces the mixing in the absence of self-gravity exactly.


\subsection{The parameter of the model}
\label{SubSec:EpiParameter}

For a cloud of a given shape the epicyclic model contains a single parameter, which compares the rotation due to the self-gravity with the differential rotation. Let $\langle J\rangle$ be the mean of the action variables of the gas particles over their mass; for the cloud~(\ref{Eq:Pulse}) one has $\langle J\rangle = 2\sigma_J/\sqrt{\pi} = 1.128\,\sigma_J$. To leading order in $\langle J\rangle$, $\Omega(J) - \kappa_0 = \Omega_0' J$ with $\Omega_0' := \partial\Omega/\partial J(0,L_0) < 0$. In terms of $\tilde{\zeta}_j := \zeta_j/\sqrt{\langle J\rangle}$, $\tilde{J}_j := J_j/\langle J\rangle$, $\tilde{t} := |\Omega_0'|\langle J\rangle t$ and $\tilde{m}_j := m_j/M_{gas}$, Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Epicyclic}) assumes the form
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\tilde{\zeta}_i}{d\tilde{t}} = -i\tilde{J}_i\tilde{\zeta}_i
 + i\,\Gamma\sum\limits_{j\neq i} \tilde{m}_j\frac{\tilde{\zeta}_i - \tilde{\zeta}_j}{|\tilde{\zeta}_i - \tilde{\zeta}_j|},
\label{Eq:EpicyclicScaled}
\end{equation}
with $\Gamma$ the parameter~(\ref{Eq:GammaDef}). It is the ratio between the angular velocity $g M_{gas}/\sqrt{\langle J\rangle}$ of the rotation which the gas induces at a distance of the order of $\sqrt{\langle J\rangle}$ and the angular velocity $|\Omega_0'|\langle J\rangle$ of the differential rotation at the same distance from the origin, where the differential rotation vanishes. Equivalently, $\Gamma = T_{rot}/T_{grav}$, where $T_{rot} := 1/(|\Omega_0'|\langle J\rangle)$ is the time in which the differential rotation dephases the cloud by one radian, and $T_{grav} := \langle J\rangle^{1/2}/(g M_{gas})$ is the time in which the rotation induced by the gas moves it over the distance $\langle J\rangle^{1/2}$. For the cloud~(\ref{Eq:Pulse}), which extends to the origin, $\sqrt{\langle J\rangle}$ is both its distance from the circular orbit and its size. For clouds of other shapes the two differ, and the argument at the end of this subsection shows that the distance is the relevant one. In the rescaled variables the initial datum~(\ref{Eq:Pulse}) only depends on $\sigma_Q$. Therefore, to leading order the evolution of the cloud is determined by $\Gamma$ and $\sigma_Q$: the mass of the gas, its width in the action variable and the external potential only enter through $\Gamma$ and through the unit $1/(|\Omega_0'|\langle J\rangle)$ of time, which is proportional to $\tau_1$. For the reference cloud in the isochrone potential $\Omega_0' = -0.08831$ and $\Gamma = 10.82\, M_{gas}/M$, and in the Kepler potential with $L_0 = 2$ one has $\Omega_0' = -3/16$, $g = 0.05627$ and $\Gamma = 7.92\, M_{gas}/M$.

The model also indicates which property of the cloud decides whether a core forms. In the picture of section~\ref{SubSec:EpiDerivation}, the core consists of gas which the rotation induced by the cloud transports to the origin of the $\zeta$-plane, where the differential rotation vanishes, before the differential rotation winds the cloud up. The distance to be covered is of the order of $\langle J\rangle^{1/2}$, and the time which is available is set by the angular velocity $|\Omega_0'|\langle J\rangle$ of the differential rotation at that distance; both enter through $\Gamma$. The width of the cloud in the action variable, which determines the spread of the frequencies and the time $\tau_1$ in which the cloud mixes, does not enter this argument. Therefore, the model predicts that clouds of different shapes in the action variable form a core at nearly the same value of $\Gamma$, independently of the width of their band of frequencies, provided that their DF vanishes at the circular orbit, such that the core has to be built from gas which is transported there. We test this prediction in section~\ref{SubSec:EpiProfiles}. We have not followed the particles of the core back in time in the PIC simulations, so that this transport is an interpretation based on the model.


\subsection{Frequencies of the mixed states and of the core}
\label{SubSec:EpiFrequencies}

The model gives the frequencies of the gas particles in closed form in two situations: when the gas is mixed, and when it consists of a compact core at the circular orbit and of mixed gas around it. In the second case the core oscillates like a rigid body, and the gas particles which are in resonance with this oscillation lie at a definite distance from the core.

Any density $n = \hat{n}(J)$ which only depends on the action variable is a stationary solution of the epicyclic model, since in this case $\mathcal{H}$ does not depend on $\vartheta$. These are its mixed states. The average of $|\zeta - \zeta'|$ over the argument of $\zeta'$ at fixed moduli $\rho := |\zeta|$ and $\rho' := |\zeta'|$ is $(2/\pi)(\rho + \rho')\mathbf{E}(k)$ with $k^2 := 4\rho\rho'/(\rho + \rho')^2$, where $\mathbf{E}$ denotes the complete elliptic integral of the second kind. Hence, the frequency of the gas particles with action variable $J = \rho^2/2$ is
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{tot}(J) = \Omega(J) + \frac{g}{\rho}\frac{d\psi}{d\rho}(\rho),\qquad
\psi(\rho) := \frac{2}{\pi}\int\limits_0^\infty (\rho + \rho')\,\mathbf{E}\left( \frac{2\sqrt{\rho\rho'}}{\rho + \rho'} \right) \hat{n}(\rho')\, 2\pi\rho'\, d\rho'.
\label{Eq:OmegaTot}
\end{equation}
The second term is positive: the gas raises the frequencies. A ring of mass $m'$ and radius $\rho'$ contributes $g m'/(2\rho')$ to the frequency of the orbits inside it ($\rho\ll\rho'$) and $g m'/\rho$ to the one of the orbits far outside it ($\rho\gg\rho'$). Equation~(\ref{Eq:OmegaTot}) only contains the contribution of the last term in Eq.~(\ref{Eq:PairExpansion}). The uniform force and the terms of the next order in $\xi/R_0$ shift all the frequencies by the same amount. This shift follows from the frequency of the small oscillations about the minimum of the time-averaged effective potential, and to first order in the mass of the gas it is
\begin{equation}
\delta\Omega_u = -\frac{G\,\overline{m}(R_0)}{2\kappa_0}\left[ \frac{V_{ext}'''(R_0)}{R_0^2\kappa_0^2} + \frac{2}{R_0^3} \right],\qquad
V_{ext}(r) := \Phi_{ext}(r) + \frac{L_0^2}{2r^2},
\label{Eq:UniformShift}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{m}(R_0)$ is the time average of the mass of the gas inside the radius $R_0$. For the isochrone potential with $L_0 = 2$ one finds $\delta\Omega_u = 0.1454\,\overline{m}(R_0)$, and for the Kepler potential $\delta\Omega_u = 2\overline{m}(R_0)/L_0^3$. A uniform shift of the frequencies amounts to a change of $\kappa_0$ in Eq.~(\ref{Eq:zetaDef}) and does not affect the dynamics of the model.

Suppose now that the gas consists of a compact core of mass $m_c$ close to $\zeta = 0$ and of a mixed halo, and let $\zeta_c := \sum_j m_j\zeta_j/m_c$ be the center of the core, where the sum extends over its particles. Since the interaction term in Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Epicyclic}) is antisymmetric in the two particles, the forces between the particles of the core cancel in the motion of its center. If the extension of the core and its displacement are small compared with the scale on which the field of the halo varies, Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Epicyclic}) yields $\dot{\zeta}_c = i[\Omega_h(0) - \kappa_0]\zeta_c$, where $\Omega_h$ is given by Eq.~(\ref{Eq:OmegaTot}) with the density of the halo alone. Therefore, the core oscillates like a rigid body about the circular orbit with the frequency
\begin{equation}
\omega_c = \Omega_h(0) + \delta\Omega_u,
\label{Eq:omegac}
\end{equation}
which is the frequency of the nearly circular orbits in the field of the halo alone. It depends neither on the mass of the core nor on its internal structure, as for the center of mass of a system of interacting particles in a harmonic trap.

The frequencies of the individual gas particles differ from $\omega_c$. A particle inside the core is also subject to the field of the core, which raises its frequency above $\omega_c$. A particle of the halo at the distance $\rho = \sqrt{2J}$ from the core, with $\rho$ large compared with the extension of the core, has the frequency $\Omega_h(J) + g m_c/\rho$, which exceeds $\omega_c$ close to the core and falls below it further out, since $\Omega_h$ decreases with $J$. With $\Omega_h(J) - \Omega_h(0) = \Omega_0' J$, both frequencies coincide at
\begin{equation}
J_{res} = \left( \frac{g m_c}{\sqrt{2}\,|\Omega_0'|} \right)^{2/3}.
\label{Eq:Jres}
\end{equation}
The gas particles with $J\approx J_{res}$ co-rotate with the displacement of the core and can exchange energy with its oscillation. For $|\zeta|\gg|\zeta_c|$ the displacement of the core changes the stream function of a particle of the halo by $-g m_c|\zeta_c|\cos(\vartheta - \vartheta_c)$, with $\vartheta_c$ the argument of $\zeta_c$. In the frame which rotates with the core this is the Hamiltonian of a pendulum near $J_{res}$, as in the theory of the damping of a plasma wave by the particles which are in resonance with it~\cite{tO65}. Since $d(\Omega_h + g m_c/\rho)/dJ = 3\Omega_0'/2$ at $J = J_{res}$, the frequency of the small oscillations of the trapped particles is
\begin{equation}
\omega_b = \left( \frac{3}{2}\, g m_c |\zeta_c|\, |\Omega_0'| \right)^{1/2}.
\label{Eq:omegab}
\end{equation}
According to this picture, the oscillation of the core can only be damped by the gas particles close to $J_{res}$, at a rate which depends on the slope of their distribution, and the damping is modified after a time of the order of $2\pi/\omega_b$, when these particles have been trapped. We have not derived the damping rate.


\subsection{Comparison with the PIC simulations}
\label{SubSec:EpiComparison}

Without adjusting any parameter, the solutions of the epicyclic model reproduce the PIC simulations of section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq}: within a few percent when the gas mixes, with a shift of $4$ to $8$ percent in the mass in the range of $\Gamma$ in which the core forms, and with differences of up to $35$ percent in the mass of the core for the largest masses and for the cloud which is widest in the action variable. In both computations the change between the mixed state and the persistent oscillation of the core is continuous in $\Gamma$.

We solve Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Epicyclic}) with the computational particles of the corresponding PIC simulation as particles of the model: they are placed on the same lattice in the action-angle variables of the external potential, they carry the same masses, and $\zeta_j(0) = \sqrt{2J_j}\, e^{iQ_j}$. For $N_J\times N_Q = 400\times 100$ and $\sigma_Q = \sigma_J = 0.1$ the lattice points whose weight exceeds $10^{-7}$ of the largest one yield $7774$ particles. The sums in Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Epicyclic}) are evaluated directly over all the pairs, and the equations are integrated with the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta scheme with the time step $\Delta t = 2.5$ ($\Delta t = 1.25$ for the Kepler potential and for $\sigma_J = 0.2$). Over the $10^4$ time units of a simulation the Hamiltonian~(\ref{Eq:Hepi}) is conserved to within $4\times 10^{-6}$ of the interaction energy and $P$ to within $10^{-7}$ in relative terms. A solution takes about five minutes on four processor cores; no radial grid is involved, and the time step is set by the differential rotation and not by the orbital period. The observables are those of section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq}. As there, a cloud has a core when the central value $\hat{F}_c$ of the angle-averaged DF exceeds $\hat{F}_{max}$, and the mass $m_c$ of the core is measured in the disk of radius $(2J_*)^{1/2}$ about the maximum of the density in the $\zeta$-plane, which is the ellipse~(\ref{Eq:CoreEllipse}) in the harmonic approximation. In the model they are computed with the action-angle variables of the external potential after the average over the radial oscillation, whereas in the PIC simulations they refer to the instantaneous potential; both differ by terms of first order in $M_{gas}/M$.

Table~\ref{Tab:ModelFrequencies} compares the frequencies of section~\ref{SubSec:EpiFrequencies} with those of the computational particles of the PIC simulations, which we obtain from the slope of their angle variable, computed with the instantaneous potential, over $20\leq t/\tau_1\leq 30$. For the mixed state with $M_{gas}/M = 0.01$ ($\Gamma = 0.11$), the measured frequencies exceed $\Omega(J) + (g/\rho)\, d\psi/d\rho$ by $(4.5$--$4.9)\times 10^{-4}$ for all the particles with $J < 3\sigma_J$, and the uniform shift~(\ref{Eq:UniformShift}) is $4.8\times 10^{-4}$. For $M_{gas}/M = 0.02$ ($\Gamma = 0.22$) the core oscillates with the frequency $0.07284$ for $2\leq t/\tau_1\leq 14$ and $0.07275$ for $22\leq t/\tau_1\leq 30$, whereas Eq.~(\ref{Eq:omegac}) gives $\omega_c = 0.07278$. Hence, the core oscillates like a rigid body in the field of the halo, at a frequency which lies above the frequencies of the halo in its own field and below those of the particles of the core. The gas particles whose frequency coincides with $\omega_c$ have action variables between $0.07\sigma_J$ and $0.08\sigma_J$, in agreement with Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Jres}), which gives $0.072\sigma_J$ for $m_c = 0.105\, M_{gas}$. At this place the distribution of the action variables has a gap: in the PIC simulation with $N_Q = 100$ the mass per unit action between $0.07\sigma_J$ and $0.14\sigma_J$ is between $4$ and $35$ percent of the one of the halo next to it, whereas no gap is present for $M_{gas}/M\leq 0.015$. The gap is also present in the solution of the model, although it is less deep. Hence, few gas particles are in resonance with the oscillation of the core. For amplitudes of this oscillation between $0.6$ and $0.06$, Eq.~(\ref{Eq:omegab}) gives $2\pi/\omega_b$ between $15\tau_1$ and $48\tau_1$, which is of the order of the time $15\tau_1$ to $18\tau_1$ at which the amplitude passes through its minimum. This is consistent with a damping of the oscillation by the resonant particles which is interrupted when these particles have been trapped, as for a plasma wave~\cite{tO65}.

For $\Gamma\leq 0.16$, below the steep increase of $|h_1|$, the model reproduces $|h_1|$ and the mass with $J < 0.2\sigma_J$ within $5$ percent; for $M_{gas}/M = 10^{-3}$ the value of $|h_1|$ is at the level of the numerical noise of both computations. In particular, the model reproduces the fact that the mixed state differs from the average of the initial DF over the angle variable: for $M_{gas}/M = 0.01$ the fraction of the mass with $J < 0.2\sigma_J$ is $0.060$ in the model, compared with $0.061$ in the PIC simulation and with $0.005$ in the absence of self-gravity. For $0.22\leq\Gamma\leq 0.27$ the model reproduces the formation of the core and the evolution of its oscillation, that is, the first decay of the amplitude, its minimum and its subsequent growth, see Fig.~\ref{Fig:ModelSeries}. In this range the model gives $|h_1|$ between $0.57$ and $0.67$ times and $m_c$ between $0.84$ and $0.88$ times the value of the PIC simulation with the same mass, which are the values of the PIC simulations with a mass which is smaller by $4$ to $8$ percent. For $\Gamma\geq 0.32$ the mass of the core is smaller in the model than in the PIC simulations by $5$ to $24$ percent.

Table~\ref{Tab:ModelFamilies} shows the same comparison for the other families of section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq}. Below the steep increase of $|h_1|$ ($\Gamma\leq 0.22$) the model reproduces $|h_1|$ within $16$ percent, except for the two smallest values, and the mass with $J < 0.2\sigma_J$ within $12$ percent. The effect of the width in angle is reproduced: for $\Gamma = 0.43$ the cloud with $\sigma_Q = 0.4$ leaves a core with $m_c = 0.082$, compared with $0.071$ in the model, whereas in the reference family $m_c = 0.337$, compared with $0.256$. In the Kepler potential the agreement is the same as in the isochrone potential. The dependence on $\sigma_J$ shows the limit of the model: for the clouds for which $m_c$ is given, it is smaller in the model than in the PIC simulations by $4$ to $5$ percent for $\sigma_J = 0.05$, by $5$ to $24$ percent for $\sigma_J = 0.1$ and by $35$ percent for $\sigma_J = 0.2$. The ratio in the assumption~(i) of section~\ref{SubSec:EpiDerivation} is $0.21$, $0.29$ and $0.41$ in these three cases, such that the error grows with the eccentricity of the orbits, as expected from an expansion to leading order in this ratio. In all the $32$ simulations of Fig.~\ref{Fig:ModelState} and Table~\ref{Tab:ModelFamilies}, the model and the PIC simulations agree on whether $\hat{F}_c$ exceeds $\hat{F}_{max}$. In the reference family this happens at $\Gamma\approx 0.16$ in the model and at $\Gamma\approx 0.15$ in the PIC simulations.

Since the model is cheaper than the PIC simulations, we have solved it up to $t = 100\tau_1$ for $M_{gas}/M = 0.02$, $0.022$, $0.025$ and $0.03$, and for $M_{gas}/M = 0.02$ and $0.025$ also on two coarser lattices. In none of the cases does the oscillation of the core disappear. For $\Gamma = 0.22$ the mean value of $|h_1|/h_0$ grows after the minimum of the amplitude, from $0.007$ over $10\leq t/\tau_1\leq 20$ to $0.036$ over $40\leq t/\tau_1\leq 60$, where the two finer lattices still agree. At later times the results depend on the lattice, and for all the masses they approach values between $0.045$ and $0.07$, which are $1.4$ to $2.1$ times the value $N_{eff}^{-1/2} = 0.033$ for particles with random angles. Hence, beyond $60\tau_1$ the solutions are affected by the finite number of particles, and the model does not decide either whether the oscillation of the core persists indefinitely.

The PIC simulations show how this oscillation evolves. For $M_{gas}/M = 0.02$ ($\Gamma = 0.22$) the amplitude $a_c$ of section~\ref{SubSec:FarCore} passes through its minimum at $t\approx 15\tau_1$, where the phase of the oscillation jumps by about $0.8\pi$ while its frequency is nearly the same before and after. The minimum and the subsequent growth are not numerical artifacts: the minimum occurs at $t\approx 15\tau_1$ with $N_Q = 100$ and at $t\approx 18\tau_1$ with $N_Q = 25$, and on all the lattices $a_c$ reaches between $0.04$ and $0.07$ at the end of the simulation. Halving the time step leaves the results unchanged, and halving the grid spacing reduces the final amplitude from $0.045$--$0.067$ to $0.036$--$0.050$.

As $\Gamma$ grows from $0.22$ to $0.27$, the first minimum moves from $t = 15\tau_1$ to $t = 6\tau_1$, and the range of $a_c$ over $t\geq 25\tau_1$ grows from $0.05$--$0.06$ to $0.12$--$0.25$ (Table~\ref{Tab:Crossover}). For $0.27\leq\Gamma\leq 0.54$ the first decay stops at $t\approx 4\tau_1$ to $9\tau_1$, after $a_c$ has decreased by a factor between $3$ and $12$. Afterwards $a_c$ is modulated, with maxima between $0.2$ and $0.35$ and minima between $0.02$ and $0.2$, without an overall decay until the end of the simulations. For $\Gamma = 0.76$ it remains above $0.39$, and for $\Gamma\geq 1.08$ it does not decay. Close to $\Gamma = 0.27$ the details of this modulation depend on the lattice (Appendix~\ref{SubSec:ValResolution}), and we only rely on these coarse features.

The modulation of $a_c$ after the first minimum resembles the oscillations of the amplitude of a plasma wave which has trapped the resonant particles~\cite{tO65}. This analogy refers only to the late oscillation of the core, not to its formation. For Langmuir waves a threshold amplitude is known which separates the waves that are damped from those that are not~\cite{aIiC06}, whereas within the resolution of the reference family we find no threshold in $\Gamma$. The state which consists of a compact core and of mixed gas around it also resembles the core-halo states of systems with long-range interactions~\cite{yLrPfRtTfB14}, in which the particles that are in parametric resonance with the oscillation of the core escape to the halo and damp this oscillation; the $30$ units of $\tau_1$ covered by the PIC simulations do not allow us to decide whether the oscillation of the core is eventually damped. Finally, for $\Gamma = 1.08$ the core is held together by its own gravity: if its particles moved independently from each other in the external potential, the differential rotation would spread the core, whose center oscillates with the amplitude $1.0$, over its orbit within about $10\tau_1$, whereas it remains compact over $30\tau_1$. For $0.22\leq\Gamma\leq 0.54$ the core lies so close to the circular orbit that this time exceeds $40\tau_1$, such that our simulations do not allow us to decide whether it is self-bound.

\subsection{Clouds of other shapes}
\label{SubSec:EpiProfiles}

All the clouds of section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} depend on the action variable through the factor $J^2\exp(-J^2/\sigma_J^2)$ of Eq.~(\ref{Eq:Pulse}). Their DF vanishes like $J^2$ at the circular orbit, and their distance from the circular orbit and their width in the action variable are proportional to each other, so that the simulations of section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} cannot tell which of the two determines the outcome. In order to separate them, we have replaced this factor by five other functions of $J$, with $\sigma_J = 0.1$, keeping $\sigma_Q = 0.1$ and $L_0 = 2$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $J^4\exp(-J^2/\sigma_J^2)$, which vanishes like $J^4$ at the circular orbit;
\item[(ii)] $(J - 0.05)_+^2\exp(-J^2/\sigma_J^2)$, with $x_+ := \max\{x,0\}$, whose support does not reach the circular orbit, as for the initial data considered in Ref.~\cite{sCjL26};
\item[(iii)] the ring $\exp[-(J - 0.15)^2/0.03^2]$, which is narrow in the action variable and far from the circular orbit, such that its band of frequencies is narrow and its value of $\Gamma$ is small;
\item[(iv)] a water bag, which is constant for $0.05\leq J\leq 0.25$ and vanishes elsewhere;
\item[(v)] $\exp(-J^2/\sigma_J^2)$, which does not vanish at the circular orbit.
\end{enumerate}
For each shape we have solved the epicyclic model for ten or eleven masses of the gas in the isochrone potential, and for shape (ii) also in the Kepler potential, and we have carried out the PIC simulations listed in Table~\ref{Tab:Profiles}. In order to compare the distance of the cloud from the circular orbit with its width, we use besides $\Gamma$ the parameter
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_b := \frac{g M_{gas}\,\langle J\rangle^{1/2}}{|\Omega_0'|\,\Delta J^2},
\label{Eq:GammaBand}
\end{equation}
in which the distance $\langle J\rangle$ in the denominator of $\Gamma$ is replaced by the width $\Delta J$ of the band of section~\ref{SubSec:FarRuns}. For the shapes (i) and (ii) the masses of the PIC simulations were chosen from the width of the band, and their values of $\Gamma_b$ lie within six percent of those of the reference cloud with $M_{gas}/M = 0.02$, $0.03$ and $0.05$. For the ring and the water bag they were chosen such that $\Gamma = 0.15$, $0.3$ and $0.45$, and the outcome expected from $\Gamma$ was recorded before the simulations were carried out, together with the opposite outcome expected from the width of the band.\EJ{The prediction is in docs/articulo/prediction\_profiles.md (commit f7cea72), written before the runs; to be cited together with the availability of the codes. It is stated with the median of $J$ and with the parameter $\mu = [A/(\Delta\Omega\,\Delta J)]^{1/2}$ of the earlier draft. For the ring and the water bag the median and the mean of $J$ coincide.} The mixing time $\tau_1$ is computed for each shape as in section~\ref{SubSec:FarRuns}, and all the simulations extend over at least $30\tau_1$.

Figure~\ref{Fig:Profiles} and Table~\ref{Tab:Profiles} show the results. At nearly equal $\Gamma_b$ the shapes behave very differently. For $\Gamma_b\approx 0.055$ the reference cloud leaves a core with $m_c = 0.215$ whose oscillation persists, whereas the clouds (i) and (ii) relax, with $m_c = 0.045$ and $0.026$ and $|h_1|/h_0$ below $10^{-2}$; the cloud (i) has a small central peak ($\hat{F}_c = 1.63\,\hat{F}_{max}$), and the cloud (ii) has none. The ring relaxes with $\Gamma_b = 0.20$, close to the value $0.19$ at which the reference cloud leaves a core with about half of the mass of the gas, and the cloud (v) leaves a core already with $\Gamma_b = 0.008$. In the epicyclic model the value of $\Gamma_b$ at which $m_c$ reaches $0.1$ varies between $0.040$ and $0.40$ among the reference cloud and the clouds (i)--(iv).

As functions of $\Gamma$, in contrast, the mass of the core and the amplitude of its oscillation are nearly the same for all the clouds whose DF vanishes at the circular orbit. In the epicyclic model, for the reference cloud, the clouds (i)--(iv) and the cloud (ii) in the Kepler potential, $m_c$ reaches $0.1$ at values of $\Gamma$ between $0.23$ and $0.29$ and $0.3$ at values between $0.50$ and $0.55$, and $|h_1|/h_0$ reaches $0.1$ at values between $0.29$ and $0.34$. The central value $\hat{F}_c$ exceeds the value $\hat{F}_{max}$ of each shape at $\Gamma = 0.15$ for the water bag, $0.16$ for the reference cloud, between $0.19$ and $0.20$ for the clouds (i) and (ii), also for (ii) in the Kepler potential, and $0.26$ for the ring. Hence, $\Gamma$ orders the appearance of the central peak less tightly than the mass of the core. The PIC simulations confirm these trends. The ring and the water bag with $\Gamma = 0.15$ relax, with $m_c = 0.030$ and $0.021$, below the values $m_{mix} = 0.094$ and $0.025$ of a mixed gas, and with $|h_1|/h_0 = 3.0\times 10^{-3}$ and $1.0\times 10^{-2}$; their central values are $\hat{F}_c = 0.00$ and $1.17\,\hat{F}_{max}$ ($0.99$ in the model), such that the water bag lies at the appearance of the peak. With $\Gamma = 0.45$ they leave cores with $m_c = 0.374$ and $0.332$ whose oscillations persist ($|h_1|/h_0 = 0.39$ and $0.31$), in between the values $m_c = 0.215$ and $0.407$ of the reference cloud with $\Gamma = 0.32$ and $0.54$. With $\Gamma = 0.3$ the ring has transported $23$ percent of its mass to $J < 0.2\sigma_J$, where initially there was none, and $m_c = 0.131$. As for the reference cloud (section~\ref{SubSec:EpiComparison}), the model gives smaller masses of the core than the PIC simulations, by $14$ to $41$ percent.

The exception is the cloud (v), whose DF does not vanish at the circular orbit. Its core is heavier at a given value of $\Gamma$, and it is present already for $\Gamma = 0.12$, with $m_c = 0.143$ compared with $m_{mix} = 0.056$; in the model $m_c$ reaches $0.1$ at $\Gamma = 0.07$. In this case dense gas lies at the circular orbit from the beginning and does not need to be transported there.

These results confirm the prediction of section~\ref{SubSec:EpiParameter}: whether a core forms is decided by the distance of the cloud from the circular orbit, through $\Gamma$, and not by the width of its band of frequencies, which determines the time $\tau_1$ in which the cloud mixes. For the clouds of section~\ref{Sec:FarFromEq} the distance and the width are proportional, such that these clouds alone cannot distinguish between them. The width of the cloud in angle is not accounted for by $\Gamma$: with $\Gamma = 0.43$ the model gives $m_c = 0.256$, $0.169$ and $0.071$ for $\sigma_Q = 0.1$, $0.2$ and $0.4$, respectively, and part of this reduction is due to the bound $m_c\leq m_{mix}F_{max}/\hat{F}_{max}$ of section~\ref{SubSec:FarTransition}.

For clouds which are compact in angle and whose DF vanishes at the circular orbit, the mass of the core thus reaches ten percent of the mass of the gas when $\Gamma\approx 0.26$, that is, when
\begin{equation}
\frac{M_{gas}}{M}\approx 0.26\,\frac{|\Omega_0'|}{g}\,\langle J\rangle^{3/2},
\label{Eq:MassThreshold}
\end{equation}
which is $0.63\,\langle J\rangle^{3/2}$ in the isochrone potential and $0.87\,\langle J\rangle^{3/2}$ in the Kepler potential with $L_0 = 2$. The mass up to which a cloud relaxes therefore grows with its distance from the circular orbit. This applies in particular to initial data whose support is bounded away from the circular orbit, as in Ref.~\cite{sCjL26}.

In summary, the epicyclic model describes the transport of the gas in the plane of the complex amplitudes of the radial oscillations by a flow which consists of the differential rotation due to the external potential and of the rotation which is induced by the gas itself. It explains why the core forms at the circular orbit, where the differential rotation vanishes, it gives the frequencies of the mixed states and of the oscillation of the core, and it locates the resonance between this oscillation and the gas particles of the halo. Its parameter $\Gamma$, defined with the mean of the action variable, orders the formation of the core and the persistence of its oscillation also for clouds of other shapes, provided that their DF vanishes at the circular orbit, whereas the width of their band of frequencies does not. It is of leading order in the eccentricity of the orbits, and it does not give the mass of the core or the damping rate of its oscillation in closed form; these are obtained from its numerical solution.
