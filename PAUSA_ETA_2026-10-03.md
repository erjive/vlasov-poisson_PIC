## 2. Main results

**Equilibria.**
- The fixed-point iteration is a contraction with q ≈ a0/(a0 + 0.35) (Wilson, g = 2): 0.017 at a0 = 0.0065, 0.587 at a0 = 0.5. It fails to converge in 60 iterations near a0 ≈ 0.7.
- The ODE construction (only for F(E)) agrees with the iteration to 1–2×10⁻⁶ in Φ_self. The difference is the h² error of the iteration.
- Evolved with the PIC code at ε = 0, the two constructions agree to better than 1 %.

**Stationarity in the PIC code** (demo 5.2).
- The departures come from an O(Δr²) error of the PIC field. Halving Δr divides it by 4. In M5 it excites the undamped discrete mode (ω = 0.1048). The same happens in L5 and L6.
- With Δr/2 and 400×25 particles there is a linear growth of the spread of J. It does not change with Δt/2 and disappears with 800×50. The user thinks it may be a discretization error of the particle loading. The mechanism was not identified, and the user decided not to run more tests.

**η.**
- η = (1 + δΩ)·(Ω̄ − Ω̄_iso)/ΔΩ. To first order it is the number of band widths by which self-gravity moves the mean frequency.
- In the reference family η ≈ 15.6·a0 at small mass, and η/a0 falls to 10.2 at a0 = 0.5.
- η orders the transition, but η_c depends on the width, W0, g and the family. The objection that η uses averages was checked. The shift is not uniform across the band, but removing the average (shift of the edge orbit) does not remove the width dependence.

**The exact criterion: the loop gain at the edge, λ_edge** (`lambda_borde.py`, battery 3.9).
- λ(ω) is the largest eigenvalue of |R|^{1/2}(−M)|R|^{1/2}, the gain of the loop potential → density → potential. λ_edge = λ(Ω_min).
- There is a discrete mode below the band if and only if λ_edge > 1 (for a monotonic F with compact support). This was verified in all 25 equilibria of the battery: the 15 damped ones have λ_edge ≤ 0.881 and the 10 discrete ones λ_edge ≥ 1.040.
- The root of λ(ω) = 1 gives the frequency of the discrete mode, to ≤ 3×10⁻⁴ of the time-domain linear solver.
- Critical masses: 0.091 (reference, η ≈ 1.18), ≈ 0.042 (J_t = 0.083), above 0.22 (J_t = 0.276).
- Near the edge, λ_edge − λ(Ω_min − δ) ∝ δ^{min(g−1,1)}. Measured exponents: 1.00 (g = 3), 0.88 (g = 2, with a logarithm), 0.51 (g = 1.5). For g = 1, λ grows as 0.15 ln(1/δ).
- With g ≤ 1 at weak coupling the discrete mode exists, but very close to the edge: δ_d = 3.9×10⁻⁴ (G075b), 4.3×10⁻⁶ (G075a), 4.3×10⁻⁸ (G1b), about 10⁻²⁵ (G1a, extrapolated) band widths. For g = 0.75, δ_d scales as the fourth power of the coupling (91 against 89 predicted).
- Interpretation discussed with the user: the universal parameters are λ_edge − 1 (distance to the transition) and g (universality class). This is the analogue of the Birman–Schwinger problem for bound states at a band edge, with g ↔ d/2.

**Choptuik and Gundlach (discussion only, nothing implemented).**
- The analogy holds in the idea of universality near a threshold. The linear transition is spectral, not a critical solution in the space of initial data.
- Gundlach's lesson applied here: compute the threshold directly as an eigenvalue problem (λ_edge = 1), not by bisection of runs. The threshold surface has codimension one.
- A literal Choptuik-type analysis would apply to the nonlinear threshold of the blob (disperses or forms a core, μ_c between 0.3 and 0.5). For collisionless matter in GR the known behaviour is type I.

## 4. Open points, in order of priority

1. **Critical mass of each family from λ_edge.** Add an option to `lambda_borde.py` that finds a0_c by solving λ_edge(a0) = 1, building the equilibrium at each a0. The wide support (J_t = 0.276) only has a bound, a0_c > 0.22.
2. **Why λ_edge/η depends on the width** (0.81 in A6, 0.97 in A2, 1.09 in A8, all at η = 0.3). An analytic estimate of λ_edge would replace η as the design criterion. One hypothesis was the relative thickness of the shell, R_e/r_c; it was not tested.
3. **The edge g = 1/2 and the point-mass background** (`BGtype = sphere`), to compare λ_edge with the ε₀(k) of Hadžić et al.
4. **Distributions without compact support** (Gaussian): the criterion "ω below the band" is not defined there, and λ_edge neither.
5. **Nonlinear side** (from the demo, Section 6.3): D5 with N×16; the (η, ν) map; D8 at the optimal amplitude; the blob near μ_c as a Gundlach-type problem (look for a marginal self-trapped state with one unstable mode, and a lifetime τ ≈ −(1/λ_u)·ln|μ − μ_c|).
6. **Discreteness growth at Δr/2.** Separating N_pc from N_rc (runs 400×50 and 800×25 at Δr = 0.05) would tell whether it comes from the angular lattice. Postponed by the user.
7. Offered and not done: an `--eta` option in `eta.py` that returns a0 for a target η (inversion by secant).

## 5. Things to keep in mind

- `iterar` now raises an error if it does not converge in `maxit` iterations. Any caller with a small `maxit` will fail for large masses, as `eta.py` did.
- The estimate of q depends on `tol`, because it uses ratios of changes above 10³·tol. With tol = 10⁻¹⁰ the q of A1 is 0.003; with 10⁻¹³ it is 0.017.
- In the hollow family F′ changes sign. `lambda_borde.py` then uses the largest real eigenvalue of MR, and λ(ω) still grows towards the edge in the cases tested (E1, H9, H3).
- For g ≤ 1 the value of λ_edge on the grid is not converged (it diverges with the resolution). The frequency ω_d is converged.
